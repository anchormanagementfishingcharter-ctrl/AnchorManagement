    Need a Florida fishing license?

    Most anglers need a Florida saltwater fishing license, and exemptions and fees are set by the state. Check the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) before your trip. Rental gear does not include a license.

    Recipes

    Cook fish fresh and to an internal temperature of 145°F. Check FWC size and bag limits before you keep any catch.

    Pan-seared pompano with lemon butter20 minutes · serves 2

    You need:

    • 2 pompano fillets, skin on
    • 2 tbsp butter, 1 tbsp olive oil
    • Salt, pepper, 1 lemon, 2 cloves garlic, parsley
    1. Pat fillets dry and season both sides.
    2. Heat oil in a skillet over medium-high. Cook skin side down 3 to 4 minutes until crisp.
    3. Flip, add butter and garlic, and baste 2 minutes.
    4. Finish with lemon juice and parsley.
    Blackened redfish15 minutes · serves 2

    You need:

    • 2 redfish fillets
    • 2 tbsp melted butter
    • Blackening mix: paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, cayenne, thyme, salt, pepper
    1. Brush fillets with butter and coat with the spice mix.
    2. Heat a cast-iron skillet until very hot. Open a window or run the fan.
    3. Sear 2 to 3 minutes per side until the crust is dark and the fish flakes.
    Crispy fried whiting25 minutes · serves 4

    You need:

    • 1 lb whiting fillets
    • 1 cup cornmeal, ½ cup flour, 1 tbsp Cajun seasoning
    • Oil for frying, lemon wedges
    1. Mix cornmeal, flour and seasoning. Pat fish dry and dredge it.
    2. Fry in 350°F oil 3 minutes per side until golden.
    3. Drain on a rack and serve with lemon.
    Grilled Spanish mackerel25 minutes · serves 2 to 3

    You need:

    • 2 mackerel fillets, eaten the same day
    • 3 tbsp olive oil, juice of 1 lemon, 2 cloves garlic, oregano, salt
    1. Marinate fillets 15 minutes in oil, lemon, garlic and oregano.
    2. Grill over high heat, skin side down, 4 minutes.
    3. Flip and cook 2 to 3 minutes more. Serve right away.

    How-to videos

    Quick help for your rental gear. Pick a topic below.

    Tie a hook

    The improved clinch knot is a strong, easy knot for most hooks and lures.

    1. Thread the line through the hook eye and double it back about 6 inches.
    2. Twist the tag end around the main line 5 to 7 times.
    3. Pass the tag end through the small loop next to the eye, then through the big loop it makes.
    4. Wet the knot, pull the main line to cinch it tight, and trim the tag end.

    Rinse your gear

    Salt water damages reels and rods. A quick rinse before you return gear keeps it working.

    1. Loosen the drag on each reel.
    2. Rinse rods, reels and rod holders with a gentle stream of fresh water. Skip the high-pressure spray on reels.
    3. Rinse tackle, hooks and sinkers, and wipe the cart wheels if you rented one.
    4. Wipe everything dry and put it back in its case.

    Reel basics

    The basics for a spinning reel, the most common setup in our packages.

    1. Open the bail by flipping it up.
    2. Pinch the line against the rod with your index finger.
    3. Bring the rod back over your shoulder, then swing it forward.
    4. Let go of the line as the rod points ahead, then close the bail by turning the handle.
    5. Reel slowly, and keep the rod tip up when a fish bites.
